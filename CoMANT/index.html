Scope and Rationale

Over the last decade, networking technologies have moved into environments that are far more diverse, fast-changing, and data-heavy than the systems they evolved from. Fixed protocol stacks and rigid rule sets are now often too limited for current levels of scale, heterogeneity, and security pressure. Computational methods are no longer an add-on. They have become central to how modern networks are designed, operated, and kept secure.

Network security, IoT infrastructures, wireless and sensor networks, and cryptographic and blockchain platforms each have their own constraints, performance goals, and design trade-offs. Yet they all rely on algorithmic and computational methods to model, analyze, and optimize their behavior. CoMANT focuses on this shared foundation, which links theoretical insights, algorithmic developments, and real-world deployments.

Security
Computational techniques underpin real-time threat detection, the interpretation of encrypted or partially observable traffic, behavioral modeling of users and devices, and automated reaction to attacks. Botnet coordination, malware propagation, and protocol abuse are hard to understand or counter without scalable computational backends that operate far beyond manual inspection or traditional pattern matching.
IoT
Very large numbers of devices interact under strict power budgets, latency bounds, and limited local processing. This calls for lightweight inference, distributed decision-making, reliability and availability modeling, and early fault detection. Unauthorized access, device impersonation, and data poisoning need algorithmic identity checks, computational anomaly scoring, and carefully engineered protocol behavior rather than ad hoc fixes.
Wireless
Wireless Sensor Networks and other wireless systems face interference, mobility, fluctuating link quality, and strong energy constraints. Spectrum allocation, interference prediction, routing, clustering, and energy-aware task assignment are increasingly solved with computational or learning-based methods, because simple heuristics break down as environments get more crowded and more mobile.
Cryptography
Encryption schemes, consensus protocols, secure multiparty computation, zero-knowledge proofs, homomorphic encryption, and post-quantum cryptography are demanding by nature. Gains in computational efficiency and scalability decide whether these mechanisms can be used in practice at network scale.
Learning
Machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, and graph neural networks now handle intelligent routing, traffic prediction, resource management, and automatic fault recovery. They push networks toward autonomous decision-making, and communication-efficient and federated learning tie networking and computation together more tightly than before.

These developments call for a shared conversation between communities that rarely sit in the same room. CoMANT is a forum for computational techniques that cut across these domains and will be needed to build the next generation of interconnected systems.

Topics of Interest

Submissions are welcome across six areas. Work that spans more than one area is especially encouraged. Select an area in the diagram to jump to its topics.

Computational Methods for Network Security

  • Algorithmic threat modeling and anomaly detection
  • Encrypted-traffic analysis and adversarially robust ML
  • Graph-based intrusion detection and behavioral scoring
  • Security automation driven by computational intelligence

Computational Methods for IoT and Edge Systems

  • Distributed optimization and lightweight ML inference
  • Computational scheduling on constrained devices
  • Energy-efficient processing and decision pipelines
  • Edge-based anomaly detection and adaptive response mechanisms

Computational Techniques for Cryptography and Blockchain

  • Optimization of cryptographic primitives and protocols
  • Efficient computation of zero-knowledge proofs
  • Parallel and scalable consensus mechanisms
  • Reducing complexity in secure multiparty computation

Computational Learning Methods for Networking

  • Deep learning for routing, clustering, and traffic forecasting
  • Reinforcement learning for adaptive control and resource management
  • Predictive modeling for failure detection and performance tuning
  • Neural approaches to traffic classification and protocol analysis

High-Performance Computational Methods for Large-Scale Networks

  • Parallel simulation and large-scale graph processing
  • HPC-driven routing optimization
  • Computational frameworks for massive network datasets
  • Scalable performance and reliability modeling

Computational Methods for Wireless Networks

  • Spectrum allocation and optimization algorithms
  • Modeling and prediction of wireless interference
  • Learning-based channel estimation and adaptation
  • Computational scheduling for mobile and heterogeneous environments
